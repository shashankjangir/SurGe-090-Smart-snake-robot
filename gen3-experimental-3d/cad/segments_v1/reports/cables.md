# Cable routes, reach and slack (Phase 1, revision 2)

Generated by `checks/cables.py` → `reports/cables.json`.

The wiring stays as it is: same connectors, pinout, ID order, daisy-chained bus and external battery lead. No
leads are cut or extended and no connectors are added. Only the guides changed.

## Route for one joint J*i* (bus lead, −Y side of servo *i* = its rear-disc side)

1. **Bay of servo *i***: both 5264 plugs hang into B*i*'s **plug cavity**, which is closed inside the round shell
   and open only at the tongue mouth.
2. Forward through the cavity and out through the **mouth notch** (7 mm wide, rounded by the shell).
3. **Over B*i−1*'s flat hub disc to the point on the J*i* axis.** The lead crosses the joint *on the joint
   axis*, lying on the disc beside the flush horn-screw heads.
4. Along the **recessed groove** in that fork arm (3.5 mm deep, 7 mm wide, below the round surface).
5. A quarter turn in the **ring groove** in B*i−1*'s clevis face, then through the **tunnel** (9 × 6 mm, which passes a
   7.5 × 5 mm plug body) into B*i−1*'s plug cavity and its bay.

The mirrored drive-side route (+Y) is the same, kept free for a future sensor lead.

- **The free length does not change** with angle, because the lead crosses on the joint axis. **No slack loop is
  needed** (previous revision: 33.1 mm loop, 9.5 mm slack change, 1.1 mm pinch point; all removed).
- Instead the lead **turns in the plane of the hub disc** by up to the joint angle (90°) at the crossing
  point. The room available for that bend is the 12.5 mm disc radius. The bend radius the lead needs is
  **unknown** (`cable_loop_r` = None), so this is not a pass, only the figure to compare with. Check on
  the fit print.
- The lead rests on B*i−1*'s hub disc, which is contact by design. The disc rotates under it, and the screw heads are flush,
  so it slides with no edge to catch.
- **Bay-to-bay lead length needed: ≈101 mm**, made up of bay to mouth, 16 mm crossing, then groove, ring, tunnel and bay on B*i−1*. The
  installed lead lengths are **unknown**. If they are shorter than about 110 mm including plug bodies, that is a
  feasibility conflict.

## Tail

- **Servo ID 10 to the board:** the tunnel from B10's plug cavity opens straight into the board bay.
- **External battery lead:** through the Ø13 end-cap hole into the DC jack. Two tie slots beside the hole take a
  tie round the lead **outside** the cap, so pulling on the lead loads the cap, not the jack socket.
- **USB-C:** a window in the side wall, which is the tail's −Z side, at the board's USB edge.
- **RF:** the shell is plastic. With the antenna end assumed at the board's y = 30 edge, the 15 mm keep-out box
  overlaps only the board's own servo headers and plugs (PCB layout, unchanged). **An RF check of the finished tail is required.**
